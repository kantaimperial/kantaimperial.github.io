Kanta Ogawa 小川 幹太

Assistant Professor · Department of Chemistry, Graduate School of Science, Kyoto University (Yamamoto Group)
京都大学大学院理学研究科 化学専攻 助教

I aim to understand and design the electronic structures of solid-state materials from a chemical perspective. Rather than treating band structures merely as outputs of calculations, I interpret them in the language of chemistry — orbital interactions, lone pairs, coordination environments and electrostatic potentials — and turn these concepts into design principles for new materials.

Research研究テーマ

02
01

Lone Pair Chemistry

Post-transition metal cations with an ns2np0 configuration, such as Bi3+ and Pb2+, often carry a stereochemically active lone pair, much like NH3. By controlling the orbital interactions behind this activity — for example by confining Bi3+ at a highly symmetric site — I tune band gaps and band-edge positions.

  • JACS 2024
  • Chem. Mater. 2023
  • ACS Energy Lett. 2023
02

Electrostatic Potential

In crystals built from charged modular units, the electrostatic potential set by how those units are arranged shifts the band energies of each layer. This simple picture explains band-edge positions across Sillén–Aurivillius oxyhalides and can even separate the valence and conduction band edges in space — a handle for design beyond element substitution.

  • JACS 2025
  • JACS 2021
03

Defect Chemistry

Defects usually limit solar-to-energy conversion by creating in-gap states that trap carriers. In the photocatalyst SrTiO3:Al, Al substitution next to an oxygen vacancy breaks the Ti 3d–Ti 3d interaction behind the vacancy's in-gap state, giving rise to extrinsic defect tolerance.

  • JACS 2025
Application

Photocatalysis

I apply these principles to particulate photocatalysts for solar water splitting.

Oxyiodide photocatalysts

Introducing soft iodide into layered perovskite oxyhalides narrows the band gap while keeping long-lived carriers; interlayer orbital interactions and flux synthesis further tune their activity.

  • JACS 2021
  • Chem. Mater. 2023

Band and defect engineering

Engineering the conduction band and defects to realise prominent visible-light-responsive photocatalysts.

  • Angew. Chem. 2025

Carrier flow and cocatalysts

Controlling charge-carrier flow within a single photocatalyst particle, and clarifying the role of the CrOx shell in Rh/CrOx core–shell cocatalysts.

  • Chem. Sci. 2022
  • EES Catal. 2023
